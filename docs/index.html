One Scenario, Every Stage

A design for running the same automated-driving test in simulation, on the bench and on a real vehicle with virtual traffic.

The problem

Automated-driving software is tested on a desktop (MiL), on its target computer (PiL), on a hardware bench (HIL) and on the real vehicle (ViL). Each stage usually has its own copy of every test, so the copies drift and results don't compare. The vehicle is the most expensive place to find that out.

Our design

The Cross-Stage Test Infrastructure (CSTI) runs one scenario unchanged in every stage. On the vehicle, virtual traffic is injected into the stack around the car's real position, and the stack cannot tell it from real sensors. CSTI has no safety role: when the stack's own safety chain reports a fault, CSTI stops injecting.

Where we diverge from existing approaches

Single-vendor toolchains, co-simulation frameworks and per-stage test scripts each fall short of carrying one test onto a real vehicle. CSTI differs in four ways: the scenario belongs to no tool (Simulator-agnostic scenario format), CSTI owns actor motion (CSTI owns actor behavior), the stack sees one frozen contract (Frozen stack contract), and vehicle injection never touches real data and leaves safety to the stack (Injection on fusion's existing topics, CSTI has no safety authority).
